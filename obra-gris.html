Structure before finish

We draw in the order a building is actually built: ground, structure, skin, then finish. Nothing reaches a site that the people pouring it can’t read.

The ground decides first.

Eighteen metres of set-back, one shadow line, the first metre of stone. The site settles the plan before we draw a single wall.

01 / Ground — Site strategy, Massing, Light study. Set-back 18 m, Levels 2.

Concrete is a drawing you pour once.

The shuttering is drawn at 1:5 before a board is cut. Every joint you will still see in ten years is decided here, in plywood.

02 / Formwork — Shuttering 1:5, Board joints, Pour sequence. Pours 4, Board 2.4 m.

Volume you can hear.

Seven point four metres of air, hard surfaces and one soft one overhead. The frame is tuned to speech before anything is clad — you can talk across it without raising your voice.

03 / Structure — Double height, Acoustic plaster, Exposed frame. Height 7.4 m, Span 18 m.

Rhythm you walk through.

Nine columns, nine metres apart, one bay of sunlight between each. The skin sets your pace, so you arrive already slowed down.

04 / Envelope — 9 m grid, In-situ concrete, Raking light. Bays 9, Grid 9 m.

One material carries it.

Local stone does the work. Oak and brass are here only for the joints, and nothing else is added — the rest is shadow and the way five o'clock lands.

05 / Finish — Local stone, Solid oak, Untreated brass. Palette 3, Stone 40 mm.

The last room is the quiet one.

The corridor ends in one room with a single deep window. Nothing is on the walls. What you notice is the light, the stone underfoot, and how quiet it is.

06 / Handover — Deep reveal, Oak lining, One window. Handed over 2026, Visits 1 / 3 / 10 yr.

The
Studio

Twenty-two people, one workshop, one saw bench. Every project is built at 1:20 in card and timber before anyone pours it at 1:1.

Build it small first

No project leaves the studio without a physical model. A screen flattens what a hand reads in a second.

One material, argued

Every project earns exactly one dominant material. If the argument for it will not fit in a paragraph, it is the wrong material.

Detail at hand scale

The handrail is drawn at the same scale as the frame. Get the millimetre wrong and the metre is only decoration.

Stay until it works

We come back at year 1, 3 and 10 and write up what moved. Buildings fail slowly; we would rather be the ones who notice.
